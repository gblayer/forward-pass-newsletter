This week at a glance5 items
01 Academia
Two papers dig into what TFMs actually compute in-context: one derives an explicit leave-one-out update rule behind TabPFN-style ICL and sets a new TabArena Elo record; another benchmarks attention kernels (FlashAttention-3 vs cuDNN SDPA vs SageAttention) specifically for tabular row/column attention shapes.
02 Academia
Several papers push TFM efficiency and scope: Support-Compiled Feature Folding cuts memory on wide tables, SwitchPFN adapts a frozen TFM to time-series classification, and a JEPA-style latent-prediction objective is tested (and found to trail value-prediction) as a TFM pretraining recipe.
03 Academia
Neural processes keep showing up as a modeling backbone for tabular meta-learning: NPBoost pairs gradient-boosted fixed effects with an NP for few-shot regression, and a causal-pretraining paper addresses posterior shrinkage in ATE estimation from tabular FMs.
04 Academia
On relational data, QUARTET improves on RelGT for graph-transformer RelBench prediction, while CRISP tackles GBDT training cost on massive imbalanced (fraud/ads-scale) tabular datasets via importance-stratified coresets.
05 Industry
Prior Labs (now part of SAP) published a hands-on guide to interpreting TabPFN predictions on proteomics data, reinforcing that in-context attention patterns are not a substitute for SHAP/PDP-style explanations.

arXiv
Benchmarking Attention for Tabular Foundation Models
Maximilian Schambach, Clemens Biehl, Sam Thelin
Problem
Tabular ICL models alternate row and column attention over 2D embedding grids, a regime unlike the 1D sequences LLM attention kernels were designed and benchmarked for.
Method
Systematically benchmark FlashAttention-2/3/4, PyTorch SDPA (efficient and cuDNN), vLLM, and SageAttention on realistic row/column attention shapes across A100, H100, and B200 GPUs, with roofline analysis.
Results
No backend wins everywhere: cuDNN SDPA is best for column attention, while FlashAttention-3 is up to 3.5x faster than SDPA and 1.5x faster than cuDNN for row attention on H100; best-vs-worst gaps exceed 3x.
Limitations
Benchmarks isolated attention kernels on synthetic tensors, not end-to-end training/inference; only bfloat16 and non-causal attention with batch size 1 are tested; SageAttention's accuracy impact is not evaluated.

M$^2$PFN: End-to-End Disentangled Alignment for Generalizable Multimodal In-Context Learning in Alzheimer's Disease
Lujia Zhong, Shuo Huang, Jianwei Zhang, Xinyu Nie, Yonggang Shi
Problem
Multimodal Alzheimer's diagnosis models combining MRI and tabular biomarkers are typically dataset- and task-specific, and need retraining when applied to cohorts with different features or imaging protocols.
Method
Differentiable prompt-tuning through a frozen TabPFN: 3D ResNet-18 and FT-Transformer encoders produce disentangled shared/modality-specific embeddings fed into TabPFN's context, trained end-to-end via a class-prototype alignment loss and a gated tabular-only shortcut.
Results
On ADNI 3-way CN/MCI/AD classification, M2PFN reaches 67.18% accuracy and 82.21% macro-AUC vs. 64.50%/81.14% for the best baseline (MMPFN); it also transfers zero-retraining to OASIS-3 and SCAN, beating MMPFN there too.
Limitations
MCI remains the hardest class to classify across all methods; support-set caching during training causes representation staleness; zero-shot transfer without support-set replacement is notably weaker than with it.

What Do Tabular Foundation Models Compute In Context? In-Situ Representation Refinement through Attention-Gated Updates
Tian Zhou, Beverly Jin, Linxiao Yang, Xue Wang, Wenwei Wang, Bingqing Peng, Mengni Ye, Jinjie Gu, Liang Sun
Problem
Tabular foundation models adapt to a new table purely via a forward pass over labeled support examples, but it is unclear what internal computation actually implements that in-context adaptation.
Method
Derives an update rule from a leave-one-out support objective, splitting it into a similarity-weighted attention readout over support states and a query-dependent gate, implemented per-block as Norm(A)⊙SiLU(W_g h) with no separate FFN.
Results
L24 model reaches 1644.8 Elo on TabArena (38 datasets), 31.4 Elo above TabPFN-v3, and 0.87173 accuracy/0.93836 AUC on AMLB29 (+0.30/+0.22 over TabPFN-v3); disabling support updates raises query cross-entropy by 0.0510 on average across 72 episodes.
Limitations
Authors note mechanism claims rely mainly on synthetic tasks with known rules; TabArena result is one fold-0 snapshot with heterogeneous reference budgets; regression is out of scope; trained weights and benchmark data are not released.

arXiv
SwitchPFN: Shared Switching Dynamics for Frozen In-Context Time Series Classification
Zhenyi Zhu, Jacqueline Pang, Peilin Shen, Tianyi Song, Tingwei Zhang, Keyi Hu, Kangjun Yin, Shiwei Pu, Yingbo Zhou, Chen Shao
Problem
Feeding time series into a frozen tabular foundation model needs a fixed-width table, but naive pooling discards temporal order and per-sample coordinate systems make local-dynamics features incomparable across sequences.
Method
Fits local affine dynamics operators over sliding windows in a shared PCA coordinate system, soft-assigns them to a shared k-means regime codebook (K=12), and feeds resulting transition/residual/signature features into a frozen TabPFN classifier.
Results
On 8 UEA datasets, SwitchPFN gets 82.63±0.45% mean accuracy vs. 79.09±0.40% for the best baseline (MultiRocket+TabPFN), a 3.54pp gain, winning on 7/8 datasets; swapping TabPFN for ridge regression costs 5.67pp.
Limitations
Authors state evaluation covers only eight UEA datasets; they flag future work to refine feature selection via independent validation and reduce computational cost (full pipeline takes 560.63s per dataset on an RTX 4090).

Support-Compiled Feature Folding: More Evidence at Lower Memory Across Tabular Foundation Models
Tian Zhou, Beverly Jin, Xue Wang, Linxiao Yang, Wenwei Wang, Bingqing Peng, Mengni Ye, Jinjie Gu, Liang Sun
Problem
Wide tables give tabular foundation models more evidence, but full pairwise feature interactions scale quadratically in memory; naive feature selection avoids this only by discarding data.
Method
Training-free inference: rank columns via support-set ANOVA into Core/Tail, split both into bounded ≤128-column leaves encoded independently by the frozen backbone, average Core leaves, and optionally add a validated Tail residual (binary-only) before one contextual prediction.
Results
Across 18 wide-table datasets and 6 backbones, matched-width SCFF cut error by up to 26.1% (TabICLv1) versus native full-width processing, with median GPU memory savings of 2.09-2.36x and peak reductions up to 34.3x.
Limitations
Tail residual recovery works only for binary classification (multiclass keeps exact Core); TabPFNv1 lost accuracy on held-out folds; TabICL backbones ran slower (0.56-0.62x) from repeated encoder calls; evaluated on 18 curated OpenML datasets, not an official leaderboard.

arXiv
NPBoost: Neural Processes with Gradient-Boosted Fixed Effects
Andrea Nava, Ken Rölli, Armin Begic, Fabio Sigrist
Problem
Neural Processes are strong meta-learners for tabular data but standard architectures struggle to represent shared structure with discontinuities or irregular patterns across tasks, since prior NP extensions mostly tweak the network rather than the underlying decomposition.
Method
NPBoost decomposes task responses into gradient-boosted trees as fixed effects shared across tasks plus a Neural Process modeling task-specific random effects, trained jointly via boosting with a cross-fitting scheme giving unbiased gradients for the trees.
Results
On 8 real-world tabular meta-learning datasets, NPBoost beat a standard NP on within-task/few-shot RMSE in 7 of 8 dataset-scenario combinations (e.g., Cars: 0.257 vs 0.266 RMSE); CRPS improvements were less consistent.
Limitations
RMSE gains did not consistently extend to CRPS, partly attributed to model selection via validation RMSE; cross-attention (ANPBoost) gave no consistent benefit on real data; evaluated only on tabular tasks, not other domains NPs typically target.

Learning to Fluctuate: Statistical Foundations for Causal Tabular Pretraining
Zhiheng Zhang
Problem
Causal tabular foundation models trained on mechanism-level effect labels reward posterior shrinkage, attenuating estimates toward the prior center rather than giving correct repeated-sample inference at a fixed population.
Method
Fluctuation-supervised pretraining (FSP) labels synthetic tables with ATE plus a weighted efficient-influence-function fluctuation term, T = theta(P) + lambda*(1/n)*sum(psi_P), deployed frozen with no test-time nuisance refitting.
Results
Continuous-row FSP cut macro RMSE 7.0% versus an S-learner across 24 nonlinear cells (winning all 12 weak-overlap cells); on NSW RCT data it lowered post-treatment RMSE from .0596 to .0522 with .952 coverage; Summary FSP ran 11.6x faster per table than S-learner.
Limitations
Deployment ATE risk stays order n^-1 even with perfect pretraining, an irreducible finite-sample floor; coverage needs accurate variance-head learning, which degraded badly under weak overlap (mean V-hat/V of only .328).

arXiv
A JEPA Recipe for Tabular Foundation Models
Mingyu Jeon, Suwan Cho, Jae Young Suh
Problem
Tests whether a JEPA-style latent-prediction objective (predict masked representations, not values) can be trained alongside standard value-prediction pretraining on a tabular foundation-model prior, since naive JEPA collapsed the encoder to a constant map.
Method
Tabular JEPA recipe: the value head reads the shared encoder's own field rather than the predictor output, and the latent target is an EMA target-encoder's full-table minus masked-table embedding difference; both arms trained to a validation plateau rather than a fixed step budget.
Results
The fix stops collapse (target effective rank rose from 1.5 to 98.1) but at convergence JEPA still trails the value-only baseline on 147 real datasets: 32:70 wins:losses on classification (.809 vs .815 mean accuracy) and 8:24 on regression (.635 vs .665 mean R2), while needing 1.42x the steps and 1.66x the wall-clock to converge.
Limitations
Authors note only one seed per arm and no ablation with the latent weight set to zero, so the accuracy deficit cannot be attributed to the latent term alone; only one model size and context budget was tested, and the latent term bought no measured robustness or accuracy gain.

QUARTET: Quad-branch cross-Attention and Random-walk Traces for Enhancing Transformers on Relational Graphs
Kyaw Hpone Myint, Nan Jiang, Xiang Li, Zhe Wu, Alexandre G. R. Day, Pranab Mohanty, Giri Iyengar
Problem
RelGT, the leading graph transformer for relational deep learning, samples loosely-connected local subgraphs and relies on a single seed-based global memory, missing broader topological, temporal, and collaborative signals across a relational database.
Method
QUARTET pairs a causal random-walk sampler (recency-truncated personalized PageRank with hub-degree capping) for denser local subgraphs with a quad-branch global module: two codebook branches (feature, topological) plus two Perceiver-style cross-attention branches (temporal, collaborative).
Results
On 12 RelBench classification tasks QUARTET beats RelGT on 8/12, up to +6.4% ROC-AUC (rel-trial study-outcome) and +5.6% (rel-event user-repeat, rel-f1 driver-top3), but trails by up to -2.7% on some churn/badge tasks; the sampler alone adds +1.6% average.
Limitations
No dedicated limitations section; authors note RelGT still wins on large, stationary tasks where dense local topology dominates. Restricted to binary classification (regression, link prediction left to future work); large tasks require four A100 GPUs.

arXiv
CRISP: Scalable Importance-Stratified Coresets for Imbalanced Tabular Learning
Hardhik Mohanty, Indrayana Rustandi, Mohamadreza Sheibani
Problem
Repeatedly training gradient-boosted trees on massive, highly imbalanced tabular datasets (fraud, ads) is expensive, and standard coresets lose accuracy once most majority-class rows are pruned, especially above 90% reduction.
Method
CRISP scores negative examples with a cheap proxy GBDT, stratifies them into equal-count score quantiles, allocates the negative sampling budget proportionally to each stratum's mean importance, and reweights kept negatives with clipped inverse-propensity weights.
Results
On a 25M-row production dataset at 95% negative reduction, CRISP retains 99.7% of full-data average precision vs. 94.2% (random) and 94.1% (CCS); on CriteoPrivateAds (85M rows) at 99.4% reduction it retains 94.2% AP vs ~90% (random/CCS) and 76.7% (CoreTab).
Limitations
Only viable when the positive class is small enough to leave a usable negative budget; reported cost savings exclude proxy-fitting and scoring overhead; evaluation uses only 3-5 seeds without paired significance tests; coreset selection can shift subgroup recall/calibration even when aggregate AP holds.
