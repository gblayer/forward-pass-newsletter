01Academia
arXiv
When, Where, How: Active In-Context Learning for Tabular Foundation Models
Christian M. M. Frey
Problem
Labels are costly, and a tabular foundation model (TFM) adapts only through the labeled rows in its context, so which rows to label directly determines performance.
Method
Studies active in-context learning with frozen TabICL and TabPFN: a diversity-based hybrid rule, Target-Aware Nearest Neighbor (TANN), and Expected Predictive Information Gain in Context (EPIC), compared with classical rules.
Results
Diversity wins the first ~40 labels, then TANN/EPIC take over; at the 100-label budget EPIC has the best mean kappa (0.578 vs 0.549 hybrid, 0.536 random) and keeps 87% of full-context kappa at 10% of the pool.
Limitations
EPIC is classification-only and ~8x costlier than TANN (145 s vs 18 s per run); only 20 benchmarks, two TFMs and budgets up to 100 labels; no adaptive switching rule tested.

Why Tabular Foundation Models Excel on Relational Data They Have Never Seen
Linjie Xu, Yiqun Hu, Jixiang He, David Wipf
Problem
Predicting a target column in a relational database usually needs a new model per task; it is unclear why single-table TFMs work on relational data.
Method
Pairs a frozen single-table TFM with a parameter-free relational encoder that aggregates columns over multi-hop neighbourhoods plus uniform in-context sampling, and argues single-table ICL guarantees partly transfer to relational data.
Results
The TFM pipeline is the best foundation-model approach on six benchmarks and competitive with or better than KumoRFM-2 and RT-J on single-table-derived databases; exact numbers are mostly in figures.
Limitations
Guarantees rest on unverified assumptions (uniform sampling, informative embeddings); column-wise encoders can miss interactions; mainly evaluated on RelBench-v1, which may be overfit.

arXiv
Beyond Foundation Models: PFN Priors Improve Tabular Hyperparameter Optimization
Artem Chistyakov, Renat Sergazinov, Artem Babenko
Problem
Synthetic PFN priors have only been used to pretrain TFMs; can they also help conventional models trained from scratch per dataset?
Method
PriorTune meta-tunes a pool of hyperparameter configurations offline on synthetic tasks drawn from a published PFN prior and greedily selects the subset with the best win rate, so a new dataset fits only that subset.
Results
On BeyondArena (142 datasets, 58 methods) tuned LightGBM+TabPrep ranks 7th by Elo, above RealMLP and TabPFN-3, at ~4.4 ms per row versus 243-3,308 ms for TabPFN-3; gains of 5-76 Elo over random subsets.
Limitations
Not applied to TFMs themselves; newest prior gives no clear gain for LightGBM or MLP; grid refit per model; synthetic tasks capped at 60k rows and 100 features.

StreamTab: Training-Free Temporal Link Prediction with Tabular Foundation Models
Mingqi Yang, Renqi Wu
Problem
Temporal link prediction normally needs a graph neural network trained per graph, and the useful signal depends on which negative candidates are used.
Method
StreamTab describes each candidate pair with interaction-history statistics and feeds recent interactions plus sampled negatives as labeled context to a frozen TabPFN-v2, with regime-conditioned negative sampling.
Results
Best mean AP on 12/13 datasets under historical sampling (median +4.0 points over the strongest published result) and 11/13 inductive; TabPFN beats LightGBM on the same features in 35 of 39 comparisons.
Limitations
Uses only event identities and timestamps (no node/edge attributes); gains shrink under random negatives; context from one regime at a time; context refresh can hurt on some datasets.

arXiv
Causal graph fusion across incomplete cohorts and causal steering of tabular foundation models
Yanke Li, Dhiyaa Al Jorf, Ayoube Ighissou, Anke Scheel-Sailer, Robert Riener, Diego Paez-Granados
Problem
Clinical data come in incomplete cohorts, and TFMs cannot take a causal graph without retraining.
Method
Fuses causal graphs across cohorts under missingness, then compiles the learned graph into the attention of a frozen TabPFN v2 at inference (causal attention steering).
Results
On synthetic data steering adds +0.011 AUROC under shift (p<1e-5; +0.029 when validation shift matches test); on clinical cohorts it gave no material gain, and the benefit reportedly does not transfer to TabPFN v2.5.
Limitations
Only TabPFN v2 evaluated; small single-centre cohorts (n=250, 266); graphs are hypothesis-generating; imputation needed before discovery.

Structured Priors: Towards an algebra of component priors for prior-data fitted networks
Thomas Bartz-Beielstein
Problem
PFN pretraining priors are hand-built mixtures with manually set weights whose effect only shows after pretraining.
Method
Proposes structured priors: named component priors combined with formally defined operators (mixture, convolution, scaling, local transform, projection) with proven algebraic laws, implemented in the macl2l package.
Results
Mixed priors from small components match or beat each component on 7/8 regression benchmarks, and on two tables reach an error with 1/9 of the data that a generic prior cannot reach with the full budget.
Limitations
Only one regression recipe on public tables; model-averaging test failed pre-registration; projection does not predict gains; each table family needs training from scratch; proofs deferred.

arXiv
Variable names provide priors: semantic grounding enables data-efficient numerical prediction by large language models
Xin-Rui Ma, Shi-Zhi Chen, Hai-Yan Jiang, De-Cheng Feng, Michael Beer
Problem
It is unclear when and how LLMs use variable names and in-context examples for tabular numerical prediction.
Method
Compares five LLMs with seven supervised models on 20 datasets, ablating variable names (shuffled, cross-domain, rescaled) and using controlled formula tasks and a Bayesian-observer analysis.
Results
With 5 examples LLMs reach R2 0.18-0.43 versus about 0 for the best supervised models; variable names add +0.34 R2 and the gap closes by 100 examples.
Limitations
Five LLMs, at most 30 features and 100 examples; only six datasets postdate training cutoffs; simple two-input controlled tasks; evidence of Bayesian updating is behavioural.

arXiv
Prior-data fitted networks for variable selection
Omar Melikechi
Problem
Bayesian variable selection normally needs posterior sampling or per-dataset modelling; PFNs are underexplored beyond prediction.
Method
Trains two PFNs, CD-PFN (conditional dependencies) and PC-PFN (parents/children in a causal graph), that output posterior inclusion probabilities in one forward pass.
Results
Scores 50 covariates in ~0.2 s on CPU versus ~240 s for TabPFN with permutation importance; highest AUPRC in all 30 simulation settings; recovers 21 of 25 established biomarkers.
Limitations
Semi-synthetic data may favour its prior; slightly worse than BiDAG on a linear Gaussian DAG; FDR control approximate; posteriors unverifiable under its own prior.

Neuralk-AI · 2026-11-06
Neuralk releases Seldon, a gated open-weight tabular foundation model, on Hugging Face
Paris-based startup Neuralk-AI published Seldon (213.5M parameters, classification checkpoint; a 27.4M seldon-mini also exists), an in-context tabular model pretrained only on synthetic data from Neuralk's own prior. The model card reports TabArena classification win rates of 63.5% vs TabPFN-3.5, 82.3% vs TabICLv2 and 38.2% vs Kumo-Tabular Large (self-reported). Weights are gated under the Neuralk Seldon License.
