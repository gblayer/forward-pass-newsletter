arXiv
Tabular Foundation Models Are Effectively Shallow
Irene Cannistraci, Julia E. Vogt
Problem
TFM inference is expensive: they stack many transformer blocks while GBDTs stay competitive on CPU. Is that depth actually needed?
Method
Replace a contiguous window of up to 96% of the blocks with a closed-form, label-free affine map fitted on intermediate activations, keeping the pretrained head; analyse per-block criticality across model families.
Results
On 8 TFMs and 171 classification datasets, dropping half the blocks costs ≤0.002 median AUROC; half-depth TabFM still beats all full-depth TFMs and tuned GBDTs. Up to 6× GPU speedup at ~0.03 AUROC cost.
Limitations
Single-block compression fails for regression rank quality and for LimiX-16M on very wide tables. Compression vs. shallow pretraining only compared at nanoTabPFN scale (1.4M params); classification only.

Test-Time Compute for Tabular Foundation Models: Mechanisms, Gains, and Limits
Kanghui Ning, Marin Biloš, James T. Wilson, Yilang Zhang, Kashif Rasul, Dongjin Song, Anderson Schneider, Yuriy Nevmyvaka
Problem
Which forms of inference-time compute actually help strong pretrained TFMs, and at what cost?
Method
Systematic study over adaptation (new DiagScale: a diagonal query-key similarity update training 0.003–0.03% of parameters), prediction aggregation, and context construction on TabArena and large OpenML tables.
Results
DiagScale matches full fine-tuning across three backbones. Greedy selection over 96 configurations cuts error 2.4% vs. default, while uniform averaging hurts. Context expansion gives no consistent gain; attention-guided retrieval helps only on some large tables.
Limitations
Adaptation plus aggregation stack but need much more compute than default inference. Context-construction benefits are task- and regime-dependent; Elo gains are not additive.

arXiv
How Well Do Prior-Data Fitted Networks Amortize Bayesian Inference?
Manuel Hülskamp, Julius Kobialka, Emanuel Sommer, Thomas Nagler, David Rügamer
Problem
PFNs approximate a Bayesian posterior predictive from a synthetic prior, but how well they do so is rarely measured.
Method
Decompose deployment error into prior, context and amortization error, and measure amortization error against a reference PPD (MCMC) under a TabICLv2-style graph-SCM prior; then test Bayesian model averages of independently pretrained PFNs.
Results
PFNs generally recover the target PPD well, but residual amortization error persists in rare settings of the prior even when scaling model size, so independently trained PFNs disagree. Model averaging reduces this uncertainty and improves predictions, up to full-size TabICLv2.
Limitations
Likely: reference-PPD comparison is only feasible on small synthetic prior settings, and conclusions are tied to the chosen prior. Averaging multiplies inference cost.

The Standardization Trap: Certifying Joint Label Processing in Tabular Foundation Models
Duong Nguyen, Nicolas Chesneau, Milan Bhan
Problem
Is ICL in TFMs explained by a fixed-weight account (features alone determine each context label's weight), as for linear regression or kernel smoothing?
Method
Show that derivative-based tests are confounded by the label standardization TFM packages apply ('standardization trap'); propose two certificates using only predictions at standardized labels that reject fixed-weight and separable-nonlinear explanations.
Results
On five public TFMs, changing one context label changes how other labels influence the prediction ('joint processing'). It emerges during training, and attention scores carry most of the measured interaction.
Limitations
Diagnostic only: it rejects simple explanations without giving a replacement. Main experiments use few datasets (four in the core grid, mostly regression-style).

Thinking in Depth: Retrospective Inference for Tabular Foundation Models
Hao-Run Cai, Si-Yang Liu, Zi-Jian Cheng, Kun-Yang Yu, Jin-Hao Sheng, Guo Yu, Chonghan Liu, Zhi Zhou, Jun-Peng Jiang, Lan-Zhe Guo, Han-Jia Ye
Problem
Predictive refinement across TFM depth is uneven and concentrated in late layers. Can depth be used better?
Method
Retro: a TFM with retrospective inference, combining Attention Residuals (adaptively reweight earlier-depth outputs) and query-conditioned element-wise Gated Attention to revise contextual updates.
Results
Retro shifts refinement earlier and more evenly across depth and ranks in the top three by Elo on TabArena, TALENT and RelArena, on the Pareto frontier.
Limitations
Likely: top-three rather than first, within the evaluated candidate sets; extra historical-read parameters and compute are added, and the gains rest on pretraining a new model.

arXiv
Closing the Context Gap: Activation Alignment for Tabular In-Context Learning
Yoel Zeldes
Problem
TFMs reprocess the whole training set at every forward pass; truncating the context cuts cost but hurts accuracy.
Method
Activation alignment: fit a lightweight linear map on synthetic unlabeled data so a partial-context 'student' activations match a full-context 'teacher'; no GPU needed, trains in seconds to minutes.
Results
On 38 TabArena classification datasets with TabPFN-3 and TabFM, aligned students beat unaligned ones at all context budgets, recovering nearly half the teacher's advantage in low-data regimes.
Limitations
Classification only and two models tested. Five of 38 datasets show a negative effect for both models, with no characterisation of why. Regression is untested.

arXiv
Compile the Table: Query-Calibrated Operator Compression for Tabular In-Context Learning
Xu Zhao, Jiaming Zhao, Bin Zhao, Yong Yang
Problem
Compressing in-context examples for tabular ICL trades accuracy against throughput: fixed subsets lose accuracy, per-query retrieval kills cache reuse.
Method
QCOC compiles the full KV cache once into compact memory: cluster states into joint-KV prototypes with multiplicities, then calibrate values against attention queries via an anchored closed-form solution.
Results
On 64 OpenML-CC18 datasets it has the best mean accuracy among compression/retrieval methods; on long tables it is 0.23 pp below full context. 8,192→512 slots (10.5× compression); up to 508× faster than dynamic retrieval and 1.98× faster than full context on CPU.
Limitations
Speedups exclude one-time compilation cost. Likely: classification-focused and a small accuracy gap remains versus full context.

arXiv
TICDA: Tabular In-Context Data Attribution
Yacine Benihaddadene, Milan Bhan, Eliot Dugelay, Mohammed Jawhar, Benjamin Wong, Nicolas Chesneau, Duong Nguyen
Problem
It is unclear how individual in-context demonstrations shape a TFM's prediction, and mislabeled or redundant context examples can hurt.
Method
TICDA: train linear surrogates on TFM latent embeddings to score each demonstration's influence in a single forward pass, avoiding Shapley-style resampling and gradients that ICL lacks.
Results
Gives the best compromise against competitors on four tasks: label-error detection, context curation that keeps accuracy at lower cost, scores transferable across TFMs, and active-learning acquisition.
Limitations
Likely: attributions come from a linear surrogate, so they approximate nonlinear ICL behaviour; 'best compromise' rather than best on every task.

arXiv
Adapting prior-data fitted networks for tabular anomaly detection
Maximilian Bershtman, Niv Cohen
Problem
Tabular anomaly detection lacks strong deep features, and no anomalies exist for tuning; the reference set may itself contain anomalies.
Method
ZEN: kNN distance on frozen TabPFN features from selected layers with a task-suited extraction procedure; FOCUS fine-tunes TabPFN on the reference set to separate normal from anomalous samples.
Results
On ADBench, ZEN reaches higher mean AUROC than every baseline without fine-tuning, FOCUS improves further, and the approach transfers across PFN models.
Limitations
Needs a GPU (~3,500 samples/min on an A100), slower than classical detectors. Scale is limited: ADBench subsamples large sets, and cost grows with reference-set size.

TAFFY: A Task-Adaptive Tabular Foundation Model with In-Context Diversity
Zijian Li, Xiangchen Song, Gongxu Luo, Jie Qiao, Ruichu Cai, Zhenhao Chen, Xinshuai Dong, Fan Feng, Guangyi Chen, Kun Zhang
Problem
TFM quality depends on inferring task-specific relationships from context, which standard synthetic pretraining may not encourage.
Method
TAFFY: an In-Context Diversity Prior (contexts from related environments via interventions/shifts on a shared causal process) plus a Task-Conditioned Looped Transformer with a task-level gate for iterative refinement.
Results
Lowest average rank across six classification and five regression benchmark datasets.
Limitations
Gating is a single task-level scalar on the final refinement, so it cannot vary per example, feature or layer. Likely: evaluation on only 11 datasets is small versus TabArena-scale.
