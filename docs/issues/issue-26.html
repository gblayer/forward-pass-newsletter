NVIDIA Kumo Tabular: Pushing the Accuracy-Efficiency Frontier for Tabular Prediction
Jingang Qu, Valter Hudovernik, Martin Jurkovič, Cedric Lorenz, David Holzmüller, Federico Lopez, Dmitry Gordeev, Akihiro Nitta, Ramona Bendias, Ardrian Wong, Vedaant Jain, Vignesh Kothapalli, Gilberto Titericz Junior, Jure Leskovec, Matthias Fey
Problem
Open tabular FMs that are both state-of-the-art and fast are rare: current leaders are slow (LimiX-2) or have closed or restricted weights. Kumo Tabular targets the accuracy-speed frontier for classification and regression.
Method
A TabICLv2-style two-stage model: column/row attention through inducing points compresses each row, then an ICL transformer (test-time GQA, SSMax, QK-norm) predicts. Pretrained on a richer synthetic SCM 'Kumo prior' with noise, missingness and a learnability filter; 27M/61M/214M sizes.
Results
Kumo Tabular-L ranks first on 8 benchmarks, but 95% intervals overlap the runner-up on all Elo boards (TabArena 1962 vs LimiX-2 1952; BeyondArena 1426 vs TabPFN-3.5 1376). It predicts 36x faster than LimiX-2 and 1.7x faster than TabPFN-3.5.
Limitations
Authors report weaknesses on grouped splits (4th on BeyondArena, behind tuned RealMLP/TabM/LightGBM) and on tables with more than 100K training rows (9th). Single tables only; text/images go through external encoders. Benchmark subsets were also used during development.

Towards Reliable Scaling Laws for Tabular Foundation Models
Johannes Hog, Neeratyoy Mallik, Steven Adriaensen, Jan Hendrik Metzen, Tom Viering, Danny Stoll, Frank Hutter
Problem
TFMs pretrain on unlimited configurable synthetic data but are judged on real datasets. Do compute scaling laws for TabPFNv2-style models extrapolate reliably and transfer across evaluation settings?
Method
Over 6,000 nanoTabPFN runs on an MLP prior, sweeping 8 hyperparameters including the maximum rows and features sampled during pretraining. Power laws are fit to Pareto frontiers up to 1e16 FLOPs, tested at 1e17, plus a study of evaluation-set selection bias.
Results
Fits are tight (R²≥0.986) and AUC extrapolates well (MAE 0.004), but real-data NLL is underestimated. Synthetic loss favors parameter scaling; real data favors more data. Selecting configurations needs at least ~8 (NLL) to ~20 (AUC) datasets before bias falls below fit error.
Limitations
Likely: toy scale (≤553K parameters, ≤16 features, ≤1,200 rows). Classification only, one architecture and one simple prior. Real-data evaluation uses only 21 TabArena datasets. The prior is not a variable in the fitted law, which the authors leave to future work.

TabBioMed: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Biomedical Tabular Learning
Pau Mateo-Bernadó, Saivenkata Nagavyjayanthi Polapragada, David Bonet, Pol Arbiol Rakuljic, Laia Mogas Pladevall, Marçal Comajoan Cara, Jesus Gonzalez-Ferrer, Jordi Abante, Daniel Mas Montserrat, David Haussler, Alexander G. Ioannidis
Problem
General tabular benchmarks underrepresent biomedical data (very wide omics tables, missingness, imbalance, group structure), so TabArena-style conclusions may not transfer to biomedicine.
Method
TabBioMed: 94 curated public datasets across EHR, omics, drug discovery and single-cell data (up to 39,969 features). Group- and scaffold-aware splits; 23 models (TabPFNv2.5, TabICLv2, TabDPT, iLTM, TabPFN-Wide, GBDTs, MLPs) with time-capped HPO.
Results
TFMs reach the top 5 on 88 of 94 datasets. TabICLv2 has the best mean rank, TabDPT the best normalized score and tuned RealTabPFN-2.5 the top Elo, but the top 10 (including TabM, RealMLP, CatBoost) are statistically tied. PCA helps TabICLv2 on 26 of 36 very wide datasets.
Limitations
TFMs time out or run out of memory on large, wide tables, so those scores are imputed. Results depend on the supplied featurizations. Likely: wall-clock-capped HPO is uneven across models, there is some pretraining-data overlap, and some model authors are co-authors.

VRLLM: Turning LLMs into Powerful Tabular Classifiers
Yingtong Dou, Shubham Jain, Junpeng Wang, Hadi Abdullah, Vineeth Rakesh Mohan, Yuzhong Chen, Liang Wang, Yiwei Cai
Problem
Can a compact LLM fine-tuned on serialized rows beat specialized tabular models on huge industrial data? The test is Visa's rare-positive transaction classification task with very high-cardinality text categoricals (more than 50M merchant names).
Method
LoRA fine-tuning (r=32) of Llama 3.2 1B on billions of text-serialized rows, predicting a 0/1 label token. Scores come from renormalized label logits, with negatives downsampled to 1:3-1:10.
Results
Recall at the operating FPR is 38.3% vs 37.5% for Visa's production model (PR-AUC 35.3 vs 35.2); averaging both gives 45.9% recall. In-context TFMs (800K-row contexts) get 3-6% recall, tuned GBDTs 6.5-8.1%, and zero-shot frontier LLMs 0%.
Limitations
A single proprietary task with no data, weights or class rate disclosed. Likely: the gain over production alone is marginal, the TFM/GBDT baselines are weak (no TFM fine-tuning, integer-coded categoricals), and there are no latency, cost or variance numbers.

PriTabICL: Differentially Private Tabular In-Context Learning with Task-Aligned Cluster-Wise Synthesis
Syed Ahzaz Shah, Evan Bramblett, Héber H. Arcolezi, Chen Gong, Qianqian Tong, Xiaochen Li
Problem
LLM-based tabular in-context learning serializes labeled records into prompts, which can leak sensitive data. Per-record differential-privacy noise destroys the feature-label structure that demonstrations need. ('TabICL' here means LLM prompting, not the TabICL model.)
Method
PriTabICL: DP k-means clusters the records, then a separate DP synthesizer (PrivSyn or GEM) per cluster spends budget only on feature-target marginals. Each query is routed to its nearest cluster and draws label-balanced synthetic demonstrations at no extra privacy cost.
Results
With Llama-2-13B on 4 binary datasets (ε ∈ {1,2,4}), PriTabICL-PrivSyn beats global PrivSyn by +1.6 F1 on average (8 of 12 settings) and earlier DP-TabICL methods by 3.9-6.0 F1.
Limitations
Gains are small and inconsistent: the GEM variant averages zero gain, and DP clustering collapses to ~1.7 clusters on Adult. Only binary tasks, one older LLM and 100 queries per setting. No comparison with TFM-based or PATE-style private baselines.

Antom 3-in-1 Transformer: A Payment Foundation Model
Weiyu Han, Haoyu Jiang, Jie Sheng, Youxi Pan, Xianglong Liu, Zeqi Ren, Dongquan Yi, Yinhao Liu, Ruobing Zuo, Ze Zhao, Huilin He, Nairong Zhang, Xinyi He, Xiao Tan, Jun Wang, Pan Fang, Wei Zeng, Jianan Su, Kaixin Shen, Jingtong Wu, Hao Li, Shuai Chen, Jiangming Yang, Tianyi Zhang
Problem
Card-payment fraud detection at Ant International spans many markets and merchants. Prior transaction foundation models encode each account's history alone and miss relational signals (shared devices, networks) and scenario-specific tabular features.
Method
A ~7B-parameter MoE (~1.7B active per task). It fuses per-entity sequence Transformers (contrastive pretraining), a field-attention tabular encoder and a frozen self-supervised GAT over user graphs, with learned task queries routing to sparse experts.
Results
In a 4-month online A/B test the loss rate fell 37.88% vs XGB-Online and 24% vs a SAINT-style model. With 25% of labels the pretrained model beats full-label training from scratch. On public IBM synthetic data AP is 0.383 vs 0.341 for PRAGMA.
Limitations
The authors withhold architecture, training and data details, and nearly all data is proprietary. On the only public benchmark (synthetic) some metrics trail PRAGMA. Likely: no comparison with tabular FMs, and baselines are re-implementations.

ICICLE: In-Context Inference for Curve-Learning Extrapolation
Laurits Fredsgaard, Michael Riis Andersen, Mikkel N. Schmidt
Problem
Freeze-thaw Bayesian optimization needs a surrogate that extrapolates partial learning curves. The PFN surrogate FT-PFN hard-codes a 10-slot hyperparameter encoder, so larger or variable search spaces require retraining.
Method
ICICLE: a PFN surrogate that tokenizes each hyperparameter with a random orthogonal identity vector, pools them with feature self-attention plus Set-Transformer PMA (width-free, permutation-invariant), and trains on an extended prior with typed hyperparameters, heavy-tailed noise and divergence.
Results
At 1.28M parameters (8.7% of FT-PFN) it matches FT-PFN regret on LCBench, PD1 and TaskSet. On 86 Meta-Album tasks with 33 hyperparameters it reaches 0.103 normalized regret vs 0.133 for meta-trained Quick-Tune.
Limitations
No clear gain on the mini and micro Meta-Album splits. An FT-PFN trained on the ICICLE prior does about as well, so the prior may drive the gains more than the set encoder. Short paper with no likelihood-based extrapolation evaluation.

Tabular Foundation Models as Emulators for Forecasting Terrestrial Ecosystem Dynamics
Zhihao Wang, Ruohan Li, Lei Ma, George Hurtt, Xiaowei Jia, Yiqun Xie
Problem
Can a pretrained TFM, with no training, emulate the ED process-based forest model, both for 40-year carbon and vegetation states and for future-climate (SSP) extrapolation, where purpose-built DeepED emulators need task-specific design?
Method
Each stand becomes one row with 158 features (mean forcing drivers, initial state, age). One TabPFN-3.5 in-context regressor per output with 45,540 context rows and no fine-tuning, compared with DeepED-LSTM and DeepED-Transformer.
Results
On 50,779 present-day test sites TabPFN-3.5 has the best height RMSE (1.62 vs 1.67 m) but is 23-49% worse on AGB, LAI and fluxes. Under SSP5-8.5 it is best on height, AGB, soil C and LAI (AGB RMSE 2.7 vs 6.5).
Limitations
The authors show its future-climate edge comes from barely responding to forcing (same 2060 AGB under both SSPs). No model reproduces ED's biomass gain. Averaging drivers loses temporal order. Only year 40, one ESM and two scenarios are tested.

TabDAG: Structured Evolutionary Search for Tabular Feature Engineering
Anastasia Volkova, Andrey Kuznetsov, Ivan Oseledets, Valentin Khrulkov
Problem
Automated feature engineering must produce compositional, data-fitted, leakage-free features. LLM proposers emit free-form code whose invalid references and train/test leakage only surface at runtime, which wastes evaluation budget.
Method
MAP-Elites evolutionary search over typed feature DAGs. Row-wise nodes cannot see the target, and aggregate nodes fit only on the training split. An LLM proposes graph diffs decoded under a per-parent schema and validated before evaluation.
Results
Tested on 10 datasets with CatBoost, TabM and TabPFN v3. TabDAG beats raw features in 26 of 30 cells, and TabDAG+OpenFE and TabDAG take the top two mean ranks. Failed evaluations drop to 20% (vs 27-28% for Python/JSON proposals).
Limitations
Pairwise differences against OpenFE and LLM-FE do not survive correction. Under TabPFN all arms are indistinguishable (p=.14). A zero-shot LLM recovers ~79% of the gain without search. Only 10 datasets and one search per cell; cost is not matched.

Gradient Boosting with Kolmogorov-Arnold Units for Interpretable Regression beyond Pairwise Interactions
Minjong Cheon, Hanseon Joo, Eunji Lee
Problem
Glass-box regressors like EBM stop at pairwise interactions and lose accuracy on targets with interactions of order three or more, which pushes users back to black-box GBDTs explained post hoc.
Method
MJBoost: gradient boosting whose weak learner is one Kolmogorov-Arnold unit g(Σφ_p(x_p)) with P-spline functions, fit in closed form. This yields an exact functional ANOVA into main, pairwise and higher-order parts.
Results
On 35 OpenML-CTR23 tasks it beats EBM on 21 (geomean RMSE -29%), but tuned LightGBM still has the best mean rank (1.49 vs 1.80). On synthetic order-3 to order-6 targets its error is up to 13x lower than EBM's.
Limitations
Costs 1.9x EBM and 6.8x LightGBM in CPU time. The higher-order remainder is measured, not explained. Likely: no CatBoost or TFM (TabPFN/TabICL) baselines, and LightGBM tuning is a small grid.

Lattice (Nanjing University / hachure-ai) · 2026-11-20
New open TFM Lattice debuts on TabArena at rank 13/120
Lattice is an in-context TFM for classification and regression (~37M params; Lattice-tiny ~10M; 8-member ensemble by default; weights CC BY-NC 4.0). TabArena merged it on Nov 20. In the maintainers' full run Lattice is 13th of 120 (Elo 1799) and Lattice-tiny 20th (Elo 1692).
YHat Labs YHat Labs releases ChakraTS-OS-10M, an open-weight time-series FM — A 10.6M-parameter zero-shot probabilistic forecaster (21 quantiles, 32k context, past and known-future covariates), complementing the API-only ChakraTS. Self-reported fev-bench skill score 49.9, GIFT-Eval MASE 0.674. Weights are non-commercial and gated behind manual early-access review.
TabArena TabArena tightens submission rules for variants and new methods — Two merged rules: a variant must improve on the entrant it builds on, and a new method needs promising TabArena-Lite results before a full run. The trigger was a Kumo Tabular-derived 'CellRidge' entry, closed after it scored far below Kumo Tabular-S (Lite Elo ~1298 vs 1837).
Prior Labs TabArena starts verifying API-only TabPFN-3.5-Thinking — A TabArena maintainer opened a submission adding Prior Labs' hosted TabPFN-3.5-Thinking as a closed-source API system. All 816 TabArena splits ran via tabpfn-client, but the default token budget ran out in about 20 minutes. Official Elo is pending the maintainers' re-run.
Databricks Databricks publishes a guide and repo for running TFMs on its AI Runtime — Covers zero-shot inference with TabICLv2, TabFM, TabPFN-3.5, Mitra v2 and Kumo Tabular, plus fine-tuning Kumo Tabular with MLflow tracking and Unity Catalog registration. It is a practical guide with no new benchmark numbers.
