01Academia
arXiv
ElTab: Elastically Looped Tabular Transformers
Mieszko Komisarczyk, Saurabh Mathur, Maurice Kraus, Sriraam Natarajan, Kristian Kersting
Problem
TabPFN-style tabular transformers are accurate but costly at inference, which blocks deployment on constrained hardware.
Method
ElTab: a looped (weight-shared) tabular transformer trained with supervision at randomly sampled early exits, so one model runs at any compute budget.
Results
On 30 OpenML classification datasets one ElTab matches six depth-specific looped models at under half the training cost, and matches a 12-layer TabPFN-style baseline with 25% fewer inference FLOPs.
Limitations
Likely: classification only, modest benchmark (30 datasets); no regression or large-scale TFM comparison stated in the abstract.

TabCUQ: Context-Uncertainty Quantification for Tabular Foundation Models
Yuebei Shi, Molei Liu, Zijian Guo
Problem
Tabular foundation models are used to estimate conditional outcomes and treatment effects, but their uncertainty from a small context is poorly quantified.
Method
TabCUQ perturbs observed outcomes with noise and re-runs the TFM on the perturbed contexts; wild-bootstrap dispersion estimates context variation, plus a limited-context bias term.
Results
Coverage improves substantially in simulations and on seven real tasks, but intervals are about twice as wide as an oracle bias-aware benchmark. Ordinary bootstrap overestimates and subsampling underestimates context variation.
Limitations
The asymptotic coverage guarantee holds for a kernel-regression analogue, not the TFM itself; intervals are conservative.

Interpretable by Design Tabular Prediction via In-Context Local Linear Learning
Ran Eisenberg, Steven Berger, Ofir Lindenbaum
Problem
Tabular in-context learners predict well but are hard to interpret, and post-hoc attributions can misreport what the model uses.
Method
TICL³: a TFM's representations retrieve similar training rows per query, and a simple local linear rule on top of a shared linear model makes the prediction interpretable by design.
Results
Across 386 classification benchmark evaluations it trails TabICLv2 by 1.1 points, beats CatBoost in 266, and improves support F1 over MAPLE by 4.7 points.
Limitations
Likely: classification only; costs some accuracy versus the TFM itself and explanations depend on the TFM's embeddings.

arXiv
Tabular foundation models are not a shortcut to calibrated clinical risk prediction: a pre-registered simulation against the true risk
Ebrahim Khaled Ebrahim
Problem
Whether tabular foundation models give correctly calibrated clinical risks was only judged on real data, where true risk is unknown.
Method
Pre-registered simulation with known true risks (58 scenarios, 1,000 designs) comparing TabPFN-3.5, TabICLv2 and Mitra-v2 against Firth logistic regression with intercept correction, then four clinical datasets.
Results
TFMs were not a shortcut to calibration: calibration slope matched the logistic baseline in 36-37 of 48 core scenarios; TabICLv2 did worse. None handled event-rate shift; recalibrating on 500 local patients largely fixed it.
Limitations
Likely: simulation may not transfer to real clinics; only four real datasets; unreviewed preprint.

arXiv
Recall or Relearn? A Mixture of In-Context Experts for Recurring Concept Drift
Zhangkai Wu, Xu Zhang, Zhihong Cui, Yiqi Wang, Yi Zhang
Problem
TFMs adapt to data streams by swapping context rows, but when an old concept recurs they must choose between recalling stored rows and relearning.
Method
MICE (Mixture of In-Context Experts): training-free; segments the stream at drifts, merges stored contexts when the TFM's estimated extra error is small, and weights contexts by recent log-loss.
Results
Theory shows recall beats relearning under a margin condition; MICE beats context-reset on grid streams, stores fewer experts than store-all pools, and has the highest mean accuracy on real streams.
Limitations
Likely: guarantees need a margin condition and pure stored contexts; a snapshot pool matches MICE under fixed segmentation.

arXiv
Meta Models: Models that Model Learning
Peihang Li, Ziming Liu
Problem
Predicting how a neural-network training run will go (final performance, learning curves, hyperparameters) usually needs costly LLM fine-tuning.
Method
Treat training records as tabular numeric data and use off-the-shelf TabPFN as the meta-model, optionally combined with Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks.
Results
TabPFN matches or beats a fine-tuned LLM meta-model in several settings with seconds of fit/predict versus tens to hundreds of GPU-hours; a pre-registered forecast was checked on a 535B-parameter public run.
Limitations
Likely: authors call it a first, humble step; gains are setting-dependent and TabPFN is used off-the-shelf.
