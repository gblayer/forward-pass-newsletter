arXiv
Two Is Enough: Binary Classification Priors Suffice for Multi-Class In-Context Tabular Prediction
Zhixiong Cheng, Shiyuan Han, Jingjing Suo, Zhengli Wang
Problem
Fixes the class cap of TabICLv2's pretraining prior to two, so every pretraining task is binary, then uses the model zero-shot for multi-class.
Method
Classification is framed as an in-context comparison between query-row and class-conditional embeddings, so the number of classes is set by the context labels, not by the pretraining prior. Binary priors are cheaper to generate.
Results
On 38 TabArena classification datasets the binary recipe reaches 1590 Elo vs 1568 for the TabICLv2 default, TabPFN-v2.6 (1577) and RealTabPFN-v2.5 (1522). On 60 credit-scoring institutions it improves AUC on 44.
Limitations
Real-data validation covers one domain (credit scoring). Elo gains come from bootstrapped comparisons with no reported intervals. Likely: the pretraining cost saving is argued, not measured.

On Data Scaling in Tabular ML: More Data, Better Generalization?
Ivan Rubachev, Nikita Lazarev, Artem Babenko
Problem
Tabular benchmarks rarely cover 1M+ row datasets. How do GBDTs, MLPs and tabular foundation models scale with training data under realistic distribution shift?
Method
Builds eight real-world datasets with up to ~10M training rows, keeping natural shifts and a random-split control. Compares GBDTs, MLPs and TFMs, and studies how validation data is used (model selection vs in-context).
Results
GBDTs and MLPs show similar scaling trends. Recent TFMs benefit more consistently from larger contexts, but in-distribution and OOD gains diverge at larger scales. Validation-like examples in context help TFMs yet do not stop deterioration as training size grows.
Limitations
Likely: eight datasets limit how far conclusions carry. The paper leaves open how to keep OOD performance improving at scale.

arXiv
Graph-to-Table Interfaces Control Relational Generalisation
Alessandro Cagiano, Lorenzo Molfetta, Gianluca Moro, Matteo Zignani, Pasquale Minervini
Problem
Graph-to-table pipelines (encoder, context constructor, tabular decoder) are usually evaluated as a whole, so the effect of each component is unclear.
Method
Matched ablations that change one component at a time, using logistic regression, CatBoost and TabPFN-3 as decoders on CLUTRR, 57 GraphLog worlds and 57 knowledge graphs.
Results
Conclusions about one component change when the others change. Absolute positional encodings fit short paths nearly perfectly but generalise worse than relation counts on longer ones. A pointwise TabPFN-3 readout with the full encoder underperforms native ULTRA on 48 of 57 datasets.
Limitations
Likely: conclusions depend on the evaluation and aggregation protocol. The study is on synthetic and benchmark graph tasks, and the decoder set is small.

arXiv
Speedrunning Relational Foundation Model Pretraining
Gabriel Wedson Mendonça de Souza Veloso, Luan Borges Teodoro Reis Sena, Denis Oliveira Correa, Francisco Galuppo Azevedo
Problem
Relational foundation model pretraining recipes are developed in private labs under private protocols, so they do not accumulate.
Method
Introduces modded-nanoRT, a speedrun leaderboard for Relational Transformers that ranks entries by wall-clock time to a quality target on one GPU. The first entry combines typed trilinear attention (conditioned on the foreign key traversed), the Muon optimizer and SIGReg.
Results
Reaches the quality target in 305.6 s on one H200, 8.1x faster than the reference implementation, and wins 13 of 15 pairwise comparisons on held-out databases.
Limitations
Benchmark validity depends on one calibrated held-out database. Reported on one hardware platform. Loses 2 of 15 comparisons.

Beyond the Vote: When Do LLMs Add Value to Retrieval-Based Tabular Classification?
Ziquan Wang, Yuxuan Nie, Jian Ma, Yingjie Li
Problem
Retrieval-based in-context learning lets LLMs predict tabular labels from similar rows, but it is unclear whether the LLM beats simply voting over those neighbours.
Method
Compares four general-purpose LLMs with distance-weighted kNN voting on identical retrieved neighbours across 19 tabular classification datasets, varying pool size, demos, prompts and retrieval representation.
Results
Distance-kNN has the highest mean accuracy at every pool size. LLMs help mainly where neighbours disagree and are moderately close, and fall behind for the most distant queries.
Limitations
Likely: only general-purpose prompted LLMs are tested, not fine-tuned or tabular-specific models, and the datasets are classification only.

arXiv
Residual-Based Causal Discovery via Pretrained In-Context Quantile Regression
Daniel Klippert, Quang Nhat Huynh, Alexander Marx
Problem
Residual-based causal discovery for location-scale noise models needs standardized residuals, but parametric estimators risk misspecification.
Method
Loscar extends RESIT to location-scale noise models by estimating conditional mean and scale via quantile regression, using pretrained tabular foundation models as plug-in estimators.
Results
Across bivariate and multivariate benchmarks it substantially outperforms an FGLS baseline with flexible function classes, especially with multiple causes.
Limitations
Likely: assumes the noise model class (LSNM) holds. Inference cost of a TFM per regression is not discussed in the abstract.

arXiv
State Estimation as In-context Tabular Prediction
Ayushi Jolotia, Parikshit Pareek
Problem
Power-system state estimation normally needs WLS solvers that degrade under low observability and bad data.
Method
Casts estimation as in-context tabular prediction: past measurement/state pairs are the context, and a frozen tabular foundation model predicts the state for a new measurement vector.
Results
On 14- to 118-bus networks, errors are 11-57x below the context average at full metering and 1.3-4.0x with 5% of measurements. Under 8σ bias on six buses, angle error grows 1.8-5.0x vs 2.5-12.7x for WLS.
Limitations
Likely: needs historical state estimates as context, and it is a 3-page letter with limited analysis. Mostly an application of TFMs, included for the robustness findings.

arXiv
Debiased Prior-Data Fitted Networks for Amortized Bayesian Semi-Parametric Inference
Valentyn Melnychuk, Emil Javurek, Stefan Feuerriegel
Problem
Prior-data fitted networks are used for amortized Bayesian inference, but their behaviour on semi-parametric targets (e.g. causal effects) is poorly understood.
Method
Shows that an idealised PFN's posterior concentration can slow as the prior becomes more diverse, and proposes two debiasing strategies based on efficient influence functions: a label correction and a prior correction.
Results
Both variants asymptotically match the concentration rates of frequentist bias-corrected estimators, with Bernstein-von Mises and minimax guarantees under extra assumptions.
Limitations
Optimality guarantees hold only under additional assumptions. Likely: theory-first and not about tabular classification/regression PFNs.

arXiv
DiffLTM: Continuous Diffusions Large Tabular Models for Conditional Generation
Alankrita Bhatt, Mukur Gupta, Andrea Montanari, Pulkit Tandon
Problem
Large tabular models need conditional generation that copes with mixed numeric/categorical columns and rare sub-populations.
Method
DiffLTM uses a single continuous diffusion with cross-entropy loss for categorical columns (with a first theoretical justification), plus new conditioning types and rare-subpopulation metrics.
Results
Reported to give strong generation quality; trained on labor data it reportedly recovers classical econometric insights. Likely: no headline numbers are given in the abstract.
Limitations
Likely: evaluated on a narrow domain, with no comparison against prediction-oriented TFMs.

Nori 1.5: ~21M-parameter regression-only tabular foundation model released on Hugging Face
New Nori variant (Apache-2.0) that predicts on new rows in a single forward pass via in-context learning, trained entirely on synthetic data. Uses a two-phase cell/row encoder and a 4-member preprocessing ensemble; AutoGluon already integrates earlier Nori checkpoints.
