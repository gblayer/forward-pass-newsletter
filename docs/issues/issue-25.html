TabOmics: A Small, Open Tabular Foundation Model for Wide Omics Data
Andrea López-García, Luis Bote-Curiel, Francisco Javier Gimeno-Blanes, José Luis Rojo-Álvarez, Sergio Muñoz-Romero
Problem
Omics tables have thousands to tens of thousands of features but few samples; existing TFMs subsample, group or compress features and pay a quadratic attention cost on width.
Method
TabOmics: a 7.3M-parameter TFM pretrained only on synthetic wide tables (a few GPU-days) that attends over all retained features jointly and returns predictions, attention-based feature importances and sample embeddings from one forward pass.
Results
On 46 HDLSS classification tasks, 8-member TabOmics v2.5 reaches the best mean AUROC (0.943), beating 8-member TabPFN-3.5 on 32 tasks with ~30x fewer parameters; a single member (0.941) matches TabPFN-3.5 x8 (0.942).
Limitations
Classification-only evaluation on omics benchmarks; single-member inference is slower than TabPFN-3.5; attention-derived importances are validated on synthetic tables, not as biological evidence.

LightTAR: Light and Fast Target-Aware Representations for Multimodal Tabular Learning
Eilam Shapira, Alan Arazi, Shoham Grunblat, Roi Reichart
Problem
Tables with text and image columns are usually fed to tabular learners as PCA-reduced encoder embeddings, which keep high-variance directions and may discard what predicts the target.
Method
LightTAR adds a few target-aware features to the PCA vectors, learned with a regularized PLS over final and intermediate layers of a frozen encoder (e5-small-v2 for text); a gate decides from training data when to apply it.
Results
Across 21 learners (GBDTs, neural nets, TabPFN/TabICLv2/TabFM), averaged over models it improves 19 of 20 text and 17 of 20 image MulTaBench datasets at a small runtime cost; gains on STRABLE string columns are modest and mixed.
Limitations
Benefit depends on unstructured columns being informative (hence the gate); uses training labels, so overfitting is controlled only by regularization; evaluated with small frozen encoders and up to 10k training rows.

Beyond Row-Level Prediction: Evaluating and Learning Composition for Reusable Table Representations
Ali Younes, Saeed Ghoorchian, Maximilian Schambach, Georgia Chalvatzaki, Johannes Höhne
Problem
Retrieval, deduplication and data curation need one reusable embedding per table, but table representation methods are judged on row-level prediction, not table-level embedding quality.
Method
A benchmark of four desiderata (partial-view consistency, label-granularity discriminability, perturbation robustness, efficiency) over 12 methods, plus SCDM, a self-supervised loss that learns only the pooling rule over a frozen ConTextTab's cell states.
Results
Lightweight schema/text fingerprints are among the strongest; TabPFN and ConTextTab states with mean/max or other parameter-free pooling trail them, but SCDM-learned composition substantially improves ConTextTab embeddings, confirmed on parent-table retrieval.
Limitations
Single-table, cosine-similarity setting; learned composition tested on one backbone; TFM embeddings depend on the arbitrary target column used at extraction; closed-corpus retrieval only.

arXiv
Experts Across Schemas: Agent-Guided Mixture-of-Relational-Experts for Cross-Database Generalization
Hiren Madhu, Sukanta Ganguly, Kiran Srinivasan, Smita Krishnaswamy, Rex Ying
Problem
Relational foundation models transfer across database schemas mainly by scaling pretraining corpora; can architecture plus target-specific features match that with far less data?
Method
MoREGT, a mixture-of-relational-experts graph transformer with row and cell tokens, attention aware of row log-ages and foreign-key names, and experts routed by column metadata; a label-blind LLM agent adds validated single-row derived columns.
Results
On 21 RelBench tasks with leave-one-database-out pretraining on six databases, MoREGT beats the Relational Transformer by +2.25 mean AUROC and -5.83 nMAE; agent columns lift AUROC 78.45 to 79.63, reported above KumoRFM-2 on classification.
Limitations
Likely: small pretraining pool (six databases) and RelBench-only evaluation; agent features add LLM cost and are restricted to single-row transforms, so cross-table aggregations are left to the model.

One Table, Many Queries: Benchmarking Generalist Tabular Models Across Generation, Imputation, and Prediction
Yuanrui Wang, Kaifang Li, Xuanyue Li, Ziyun Li, Yan Lu, Xingxuan Zhang, Peng Cui
Problem
Tabular generation, imputation and prediction query the same joint distribution but are benchmarked separately, so it is unclear whether one generalist model can do all three.
Method
One Table Benchmark: 56 datasets, 46 methods, 20 capability axes under aligned splits and explicit task contracts, plus a factorial study training five backbones under all seven objective combinations.
Results
No model wins everywhere: TabPFN-3 leads numerical point imputation and TSTR gap, TabICLv2 leads distribution/association preservation, TabSyn leads categorical axes, LimiX-2 leads prediction; adding the recipient's own objective gives the largest gains.
Limitations
Conclusions depend on chosen axes and contracts; factorial study covers only five datasets (one regression); auxiliary-objective effects are exploratory and vary by backbone; rank alignment is correlational, not transfer.

arXiv
FADO: Learning Causal Explanations with Prior-Fitted Networks
Seth Flaxman, Jess Carr, Annabel Jakob, Marek Masiak, Dino Sejdinovic
Problem
SHAP and permutation importance answer predictive, correlational questions; scientists often want to know whether intervening on a feature would change the outcome.
Method
FADO trains a prior-fitted network on synthetic SCM-generated tables to predict each feature's intervention effect E[Y|do(Xi=+1)] - E[Y|do(Xi=-1)] directly from an observational dataset, amortizing causal explanation.
Results
Gives better causal feature rankings than predictive attribution under proxies, confounders and colliders, and correctly zeroes proxy and noise decoys.
Limitations
Learns a posterior mean under its prior, so it fails where effects are unidentifiable (linear-Gaussian, hidden confounders); ranks features reliably but point effect sizes are inaccurate; main model trained up to 15 features (64 in appendix).

MediPFN: Amortized In-Context Nuisance Learning and Inference for Causal Mediation Analysis
Shi Bo, Jiaming Hu
Problem
Causal mediation analysis needs several nuisance models and honest confidence intervals; bootstrap uncertainty around flexible learners is slow and often miscalibrated.
Method
MediPFN adapts a pretrained TFM to nuisance prediction inside a cross-fitted influence-function estimator, with a separate head trained on simulated tables to learn quantiles of the estimator's error, then fine-tuning, symmetrization and calibration.
Results
Coverage of 94.0%, 93.0% and 91.7% (nominal 95%) across three synthetic settings, intervals ~84-91% shorter than plain TabPFN v3 (which over-covers at 99.7%), and 59.1x faster than bootstrap.
Limitations
Coverage guarantees rest on standard identification assumptions and on the simulated prior matching real data; slightly under-covers under composition shift; Likely: limited to settings resembling its simulated mediation designs.

Out-of-the-Box Tabular Foundation Models for Bayesian Optimization in Materials Discovery
Klemens Flöge, Simon Bing, Leo Grinsztajn, Eliott Kalfon, Lennart Purucker, Noah Hollmann, Frank Hutter
Problem
Bayesian optimization in materials discovery relies on Gaussian-process surrogates that need task-specific kernel choices and may lack capacity for complex relationships.
Method
Uses TabPFN and TabICL out of the box (no kernel selection or active-learning-specific training) as BO surrogates, compared with GPs, random forests and CatBoost on two materials benchmarks.
Results
Both TFMs are highly competitive surrogates across the two benchmarks against a broad baseline field, without any task-specific tuning.
Limitations
Retrospective replay on small pools with few variables, so it does not show which data a TFM would collect live; pools were partly gathered by BO-guided campaigns; ranking depends on benchmark-specific metrics.

An Evaluation of Tabular Foundation Models as Training-Free World Models
Xian Yeow Lee, Ryoichi Takase, Ahmed Farahat
Problem
World models for model-based RL need substantial task-specific interaction data; can a pretrained TFM act as a training-free dynamics model?
Method
Frames next-state prediction as in-context regression over transition pairs with a TFM (TabFM), and tests one-step and autoregressive rollout accuracy on synthetic dynamical systems and robot locomotion.
Results
Beats LSTMs and MLPs trained on the same few transitions almost everywhere and is the best fitted regressor on locomotion (GPs win on smooth low-dimensional systems); fully trained models remain clearly better, and short-context rollouts degrade to trivial baselines.
Limitations
About three orders of magnitude slower than an LSTM; explicit-state predictors only (no closed-loop or pixel control); single seed; long-horizon quality hinges on context length.

GenPFN: An In-Context Tabular Foundation Model Approach for Augmenting Small Datasets in Engineering
Noah J. Bagazinski, Cyril Picard, Faez Ahmed
Problem
Engineering design datasets are small because simulations and experiments are costly; tabular generators like CTGAN or TabDDPM need training data that is scarce.
Method
GenPFN uses a frozen TabPFN classifier as a guide: candidates initialized between desired designs move along clipped classifier gradients plus Gaussian noise, are checked against constraints and objectives, and feasible ones augment the context.
Results
Over 14 problems (up to a 44-parameter hull), it gives higher class satisfaction and lower realism distance than CTGAN, TVAE and TabDDPM; augmentation cuts AutoGluon validation MSE by 62.1% vs context-only training.
Limitations
No systematic tuning of step, clipping and noise settings, which are problem-sensitive; guidance uses class membership only, not continuous objectives; TVAE/CTGAN give more coverage and TVAE/TabDDPM lower downstream MSE.

Ant Group · 2026-11-13
Ant Group releases Inpriora, an open tabular FM, and submits it to TabArena
Inpriora is a synthetic-data-pretrained in-context tabular FM (118M classifier, 121M regressor; Apache-2.0) with Fourier cell encodings and separate context/query attention. Weights: huggingface.co/ai-for-data/inpriora. Its self-run TabArena evaluation reports Elo 1823.6 (rank 6/109; 4th on regression); the submission PR is still under review.
LimiX (Stable AI) Retrained LimiX-2-1016 replaces contaminated LimiX-2 on TabArena — After a TabArena issue showed LimiX-2 had seen labelled data from two benchmark datasets, the LimiX team fixed two pipeline bugs and retrained the late stage on synthetic data only. The new checkpoint was merged on Nov 13 and the old LimiX-2 entry marked superseded.
TabArena TabArena adds transduction and contamination audits — Following the LimiX case, TabArena added an audit_transduction check and requires submitted systems to predict inductively (PR #722); a separate open community PR (#723) proposes a black-box test-set contamination audit.
Neuralk-AI Seldon verified on BeyondArena: 4th overall, weaker off IID splits — The maintainers' BeyondArena run ranks Seldon 4th of 42 methods (Elo 1358) and 2nd on IID splits, but 17th on temporal, 18th on grouped and 16th on large datasets; Seldon-Mini places 8th overall.
Prior Labs DeepLearning.AI short course on TabPFN for data-science agents — A free 1.5-hour course taught by Frank Hutter, Eliott Kalfon and Tuana Çelik on using TabPFN for predictions and handing them to an LLM agent.
Continuous Intelligence ci-proto: Mamba-3-based time-series FM with open weights — A UK startup's time-series FM family using linear recurrences (Mamba-3) with cross-variate attention, multivariate probabilistic forecasting with covariates and a 2M+ step context; it reports a new state of the art among TSFMs on fev-bench (paper: arXiv 2611.16934).
Abel AI Lab Simons: open time-series FM family in four sizes (25M-297M) — Zero-shot probabilistic forecasting with known-future covariates, released with training data. The Nov 11 release post claims 4th among open-weight models on GIFT-Eval, fev-bench and TIME and ~20x faster inference, trained for under $8k of GPU time.
